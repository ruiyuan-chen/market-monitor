Commodities, currencies and crypto

Oil, gold, the dollar and Bitcoin.

Teaching notes Why other markets matter for stocks

Oil

WTI crude is the U.S. benchmark, quoted here as the front-month futures price. Oil prices feed straight into headline inflation and Energy-sector earnings. Sharp spikes have preceded several recessions.

Gold

Gold pays no income, so it tends to do well when real interest rates fall and during periods of fear. Central-bank purchases also move its price.

The dollar

The Dollar Index (DXY) measures the dollar against six currencies, mostly the euro. A stronger dollar lowers the dollar value of U.S. multinationals' foreign sales. EUR/USD is quoted as dollars per euro, so when it rises the dollar is weaker.

Bitcoin

Bitcoin trades around the clock and is far more volatile than stocks. Its price is quoted as of the time of the update.
